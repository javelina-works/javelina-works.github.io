# Sources audit: "Chemical weed and brush control: what the herbicides actually do"

Every source below was opened during the drafting session. PDFs that WebFetch returned as binary were saved locally and converted with `pdftotext`; "fetched full text" means the complete document text was read that way. Where a WebFetch summary disagreed with the PDF text (Pronone and Sendero summaries both misreported the active ingredient percentages), the PDF text was used and the summary discarded.

## Claims considered and dropped because a source could not be opened

- Current per-acre or per-tree treatment costs for any method. The only cost figures opened were Ueckert's hand-grubbing costs ($3.65 to $9.00 per acre), which are decades old, and E-44's qualitative "lower costs" for fire. No dollar figures were used.
- A landowner-survey comparison of perceived costs (mechanical and aerial most expensive, fire least). The Journal of Range Management article redirected to a new journal host and could not be opened, so the claim was cut.
- A statement that 2,4-D is a "regulated herbicide" under Texas rules. The TDA pages opened do not define regulated herbicides; the article instead cites the Texas A&M mode-of-action guide's "requires a license to buy" column.
- Oklahoma State and UC ANR mode-of-action pages (403 and 404 errors). The Texas A&M mode-of-action guide covers the same ground and was used instead.
- A sentence that systemic soil herbicides kill the redberry bud zone directly. No opened source says this in so many words; the article now says the demonstrations measured root kill, which is sourced.
- The Brush Busters cedar leaflet (L-5160) itself returned 404 at every URL tried; the texnat.tamu.edu cedar index page was used for the top-removal and 7-of-10 statements.
- A Texas A&M "Juniper Biology and Management in Texas" (B-6074) landing page was opened but only describes the publication; the PDF was not opened and nothing is cited to it.
- Current Tordon 22K label (restricted-use statement, grazing intervals). Not opened; restricted-use status and picloram grazing limits are cited to the EPA picloram RED instead.

## Citations I am less than fully confident in

- [^5] ERM-1466, 2026 edition: the PDF text was extracted from a wide table layout, so column alignment had to be reconstructed. Every number quoted was cross-checked against the same row in the 2018 edition, where the wording matched, but an editor should spot-check the Ashe juniper, redberry juniper, mesquite and Davis Mountains rows against the rendered PDF.
- [^7] E-44 treatment-life table: the chaining rows read "2-3" and "4-5" in extracted text; the article says "two to five years depending on the pass" on the assumption the second row is two-way chaining, which the extraction did not label clearly.
- [^8] Spike 20P label: the EPA-stamped copy opened is dated December 2006. The current specimen label (fbn.com) returned 403. Rates and restrictions quoted may have been revised since.
- [^13] Ueckert's picloram figures (82% and 65%) come from a web summary of the symposium chapter produced by the fetch tool rather than the raw page text; the figures are consistent with the rest of the chapter but were not read verbatim.
- [^14] Scifres 1972: only the abstract was read, on a third-party rangeland library page, not the journal site.
- [^22] Sendero: the document opened is the 2020 supplemental label for hay sold off the ranch in NM, OK and TX, not the master container label. The grazing, hay, manure and tree-root statements are on that supplemental label; mesquite rates and timing are cited to ERM-1466 and Brush Busters instead.
