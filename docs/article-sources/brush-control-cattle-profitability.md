## Claims considered and removed because no source could be opened

- **Teague, Ansley, Kreuter, Pinchak and McGrann 2001, Journal of Range Management 54(5):553-560 ("Economics of managing mesquite in north Texas: a sensitivity analysis").** The brief asked for this paper specifically. The journals.uair.arizona.edu PDF link redirects to a new platform root; repository.arizona.edu returns 403; BioOne's JRM issue page returned HTTP 500; ResearchGate was not attempted (not a primary page). I cited instead the same authors' Rangelands 23(6):9-14 (December 2001) article, which presents the same analysis (same 20,000-acre ranch, same NPV/B-C tables, same $57/ha = $23/ac herbicide cost) and cites the JRM paper. The editor may want to swap or add the JRM citation; the numbers in the article match the Rangelands text exactly, and search snippets of the JRM abstract agree ("herbicide would have to be less than half the current cost of $57 per hectare").
- **Lemberg, Mjelde, Conner, Stuth, Griffin and Rosenthal 2002, JAWRA 38(2):409-422.** Wiley returned 403 on both the DOI page and the abstract URL. Search snippets say brush control raised water yield on 35 percent of the Frio basin but cost more than the rancher returns on most range sites. Not cited.
- **Thurow and Hester 1997 (juniper interception up to 70 percent) and Dye, Ueckert and Whisenant 1995 (Nolan County juniper-forage curve).** Not opened directly. The Nolan County curve is cited through McGinty 1997, which summarizes it; the sentence about the 34 percent canopy inflection was cut in the final length edit anyway. Thurow's interception figure appears in Wilcox 2002, but I did not use it.
- **USDA NRCS EQIP program page, Texas EQIP page, Texas payment schedule, and the Brush Management (314) conservation practice standard PDF.** nrcs.usda.gov timed out on every attempt (seven tries across four URLs); eCFR redirected to a bot-block page; farmers.gov returned 404; eFOTG returned 403. The article therefore describes EQIP through Johnson 2004 (a Texas A&M Extension economist's description of the Texas program) and explicitly tells readers that rates change yearly and to get current numbers from the NRCS field office. No current EQIP percentage or per-acre rate is stated. The FY2023 Texas EQIP payment schedule PDF (brush management scenarios in the roughly $37 to $326 per acre range per search snippets) could not be opened and is not cited.
- **Texas A&M AgriLife "Juniper Biology and Management in Texas" (B-6074).** The AgriLife library page loaded but contains only a blurb; the PDF is behind AgriLife Learn. Not cited.
- **Land value effect of brush control.** TRERC's "Ground Control" article (Tian Su, 2024) was opened and contains nothing on brush or recreation. No reputable source isolating a brush-control effect on sale price was found, so the article says so and makes no claim.
- **Hunting lease rate surveys.** No AgriLife or TRERC survey page could be found and opened; the only agency figure used is TPWD's 2012 short-term lease brochure ($2 to $10/ac). Commercial lease-marketplace pages with higher per-acre figures were deliberately not used.
- **Teague et al. 2008, Ecological Economics (fire and grazing simulation model).** ScienceDirect returned 403. Not cited.
- **Scifres, Hamilton et al. 1983 Integrated Brush Management Systems bulletin (B-1450, OakTrust).** OakTrust returned 403. IBMS points are cited through Hamilton and Conner 2004 instead.

## Citations the editor should double-check

- **[^11] Whitson and Scifres 1981.** The rangelandsgateway.org landing page gave the citation (JRM 34(5):415-420) and a summary of the rates of return; I did not see the full PDF. The specific figures (9.6 to 17.9 percent herbicide, 7.1 to 12.5 percent chaining, $37 to $47.50/cwt, 1978 dollars) come from that page's summary. No DOI is given because I could not confirm one.
- **[^8] Johnson 2004, EQIP cost-share percentages.** These are 2004 Texas figures (and the "generally 40 to 50 percent, some practices up to 75 percent" statement is from the same 2004 text). The article labels them as the Extension economist's description of the program and tells readers rates change yearly. If the editor can open the current Texas payment schedule, a current figure would be better.
- **[^18] Medlin et al. 2019, $109.30/ha.** The Cambridge abstract page summary gave "~$109.30/ha for mesquite" follow-up cost in 2016 dollars. I converted to roughly $44 per acre (109.30 / 2.471 = 44.2). The abstract summary did not state the exact wording around this figure, so treat the per-hectare number as approximate (the article says "about").
- **[^13] Hamilton and Conner 2004, chaining cost.** The text says chaining was estimated at $20/ac and burns at $5 and $4, with a 50 percent cost share; their Table 3 shows the owner's share ($10, $2.50, $2.00). The article states the gross costs and the 50 percent share, which matches the text.
- **[^20] TPWD brochure date.** The brochure is coded "3/12"; I state it as 2012.
- **[^6] "20 to 26 inch country."** The 26-inch figure is Ansley's Vernon site (66.5 cm); the 20-inch figure is Wilcox's North Concho average (~500 mm). Both are in the cited texts; the summary phrase is mine.
- **Low-rainfall caveat.** The article attributes the slow-response claim to Richardson (TPWD 2003) and to the rainfall dependence in Ansley 2001 and Teague 2001. No study measuring forage response to brush control in the Trans-Pecos specifically was opened; the article does not claim one.
