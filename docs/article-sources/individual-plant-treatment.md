## Claims removed because I could not open a source

- McGinty and Ueckert 2001, "The Brush Busters success story," Rangelands 23(6): 3-8. The Arizona repository (403) and the old journals.uair.arizona.edu URL (redirect) would not open; the Rangelands Gateway record gave only the citation and keywords, no abstract. I dropped the sentence "They wrote it up in Rangelands in 2001" rather than cite a catalogue record. Program history is cited instead to the 1997 symposium paper by the same authors (footnote 6), which did open.
- Teague et al. 2001, "Economics of managing mesquite in north Texas: a sensitivity analysis," J. Range Manage. 54(5): 553-560. Search snippets mention IPT at about $30/ha for 1,000-1,200 plants/ha, but the repository page returned 403. Not cited.
- Texas EQIP FY2026 "Chemical Control, Individual Plant Treatment" scenario rate ($49.17 per acre in a search snippet). The nrcs.usda.gov PDFs timed out repeatedly and the azureedge copy of the STAC deck did not contain that rate in its text layer. Not cited; the article uses the CSP FY24 scenario names and density classes only, without dollar amounts.
- Texas Tech thesis "Economic feasibility of redberry juniper control using individual tree treatments" (ttu-ir.tdl.org): 403. Would have been a good labor-share source. Not cited.
- Current Brush Busters cedar leaflet (How to Master Cedar, 2021 reissue) and the 2020 mesquite cost sheet (E-131): the southtexasrangelands, kerr, ector and colorado county mirrors all 404 or redirect, and the OAKTrust bitstream returned 403. The texnat method pages (footnotes 5, 9, 29, 30) carry the same content and were used instead.
- EPA-stamped Pronone Power Pellet label (www3.epa.gov PPLS, 2003): the file is a scanned JBIG2 image with an unreliable OCR layer. I cite the Pro-Serve specimen label hosted at domyown.com instead (footnote 19), which is text and matches the EPA copy on the rainfall, dripline and 600-pellet statements where the OCR was legible.

## Citations I am less than fully confident in

- Footnote 6 and 22 (Juniper Symposium): the texnat pages do not print a year. The Juniper Symposium at San Angelo was held in January 1997 (Technical Report 97-1); I have given 1997 for both but could not confirm the date from the pages themselves. The content and author attributions are verified.
- Footnote 15 (Behind the Dozer): the proceedings have no single author. I attribute specific claims in the article to "the San Angelo center's review." Walker wrote the efficacy and supervision sections, Ueckert and McGinty wrote the maintenance section, and the 300-400 plants/acre figure is cited there to Ueckert and McGinty 1999, which I did not open; the article attributes it to the 2004 review, not to the 1999 source.
- Footnote 18 (pricklypear efficacy): the report carries no publication date; trials were established in 2004 and evaluated in 2005 and 2006. I cite it as n.d. with the trial years.
- Footnote 7 (Kreuter et al. 2001): opened as the Rangelands Gateway abstract record, not the journal page; the DOI is from that record.
- Footnote 27 is a Conservation Stewardship Program payment file, not an EQIP file. The article says "Texas NRCS payment schedules" and uses it only for scenario names and density classes, which also appear in the EQIP scenario list per search snippets I could not open. An editor may want to confirm the EQIP scenario names at the local field office.
- Footnote 26 (NMSU B-817): the page says revised November 2025; cited as 2025.
- The "about a million acres" conversion of 405,000 ha is mine (405,000 ha is 1,000,800 acres).

## Notes on the Javelina Works paragraph

The aerial IPT paragraph uses only the brief's facts: drone survey at 2.5 to 5 cm per pixel, counts and maps every woody plant, owner marks the trees to keep (oaks or piñons named as examples of an option, not a default), target list triple-checked, hexazinone pellets placed at each target from the air, firm quote from the survey. No results, acreages, kill rates or prices are claimed.
