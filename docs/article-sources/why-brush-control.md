- fetched full text = the PDF or page was opened with WebFetch and the full article text was read (several PDFs came back as binary from WebFetch and were converted locally with pdftotext from the file WebFetch saved; those are still marked "fetched full text")
- fetched abstract/landing page = only the abstract or a landing page with the abstract was opened

## Claims removed because I could not open a source

- Archer, Schimel and Holland 1995 (Climatic Change 29: 91-99) on CO2 and shrubland expansion. Springer's landing page redirected into a cookie loop and the Archer lab index returned 404. The CO2 point is carried by Polley et al. 2002 instead, and Brown and Archer 1999 (opened) lists CO2 among the suggested drivers.
- Wilcox and Huang 2010, "Woody plant encroachment paradox" (Geophysical Research Letters 37: L07402). Wiley returned 403 and the ADS abstract page returned 405. The finding (Edwards Plateau baseflow rose over 85 years while woody cover increased) is described by the same first author in the opened 2014 comment letter, which is cited for it at [^19]. I did not state the "baseflow doubled" figure because I could not open the paper.
- Bryant, Launchbaugh and Koerth 1983, "Controlling mature ashe juniper in Texas with crown fires" (J. Range Manage. 36: 165-168). The specific crown-fire conditions (wind over 10 mph, canopy over 35%, RH 20 to 40%, fires stopping at 23 to 33 ft spacing) appeared only in a search snippet; the Arizona repository returned 403. Replaced with the FEIS review's 26-foot spacing figure, which I did open.
- Mesquite pod removal by cattle, feral hogs and native herbivores (Rangeland Ecol. Manage. 2017). Both ScienceDirect and the Arizona repository returned 403. Cattle dispersal is carried by Ansley et al. 1997 and Brown and Archer 1999.
- The "99.3 million acres of Texas rangeland infested by 50 noxious brush species (1982 Texas Brush Inventory)" figure appeared in a search snippet attributed to Texas Tech work, but none of the documents I opened contained it. Not used.
- The "mesquite occupies 56 million acres in Texas" figure (often attributed to Scifres 1980) was not in any document I opened. Not used; the NRI percentages from the TSSWCB plan are used instead.
- Bobwhite 5 to 15% low shrub canopy guidance (TPWD Panhandle page says 5 to 25%; the AgriLife bobwhite habitat guide EWF-020 PDF was too large to fetch). I used Lyons and Ginnett 1998 (about 15% minimum) instead.
- Texas Rural Land Value Trends (Trans-Pecos per-acre values, hunting lease rates). The current reports require email registration; the AgriLife blog post had no numbers. No land price figures are stated in the article.
- Fire Ecology 2018 and 2024 papers on Ashe juniper live fuel moisture and crown fire susceptibility (Springer cookie loop). The 100% / 80% live fuel moisture thresholds from the snippet were not used.
- Mitchell, Britton, Wester and Mullins (Texas Tech redberry juniper exclosure data, 1,411 vs 101 junipers per acre ungrazed vs grazed). Page was opened, but the result runs against the grazing narrative without an explanation on the page, so I left it out rather than explain it without a source.
- A Fort Davis or Davis Mountains average annual rainfall figure. I did not find one in an opened source, so the article only gives the 500 mm (about 20 in) threshold, the 17 in state threshold, and the 8 in near El Paso figure, and says the remaining healthy savannas sit at the higher, wetter elevations (Richardson 2003).

## Citations I am less than fully confident in

- [^7] Ansley et al. 1995: the pdftotext output of the scanned Rangelands PDF reads "5.3 million acres (2.5 million ha)" for 1948 and "51 percent increase." 2.5 million ha is 6.2 million acres, and both FEIS (Hauser 2007) and McGinty 1997 report 6.3 million acres and a 60 to 61 percent increase, so I believe the "5.3" and "51" are OCR errors for "6.3" and "61." The article states "about 6.3 million acres in 1948 to 10.1 million in 1982, roughly a 60 percent increase" and cites both [^7] and [^11]. Worth a glance at the original PDF.
- [^9], [^14], [^25], [^26], [^38] (1997 Juniper Symposium chapters): the symposium index page gives the year (1997) and editor (C.A. Taylor Jr.) but no technical report number. Individual chapter pages did not all print a year. If the editor wants the report number, it is usually cited as Texas Agricultural Experiment Station Technical Report 97-1.
- [^2], [^3], [^30], [^33], [^39] (Brush Sculptors chapters): symposium dates (Aug 21-22 and Sept 17-18, 1997) and editors come from the index page; chapter author names come from each chapter page.
- [^13] TWDB 2000 assessment: the "under 17 inches or over 32 inches" judgment is attributed in the report itself to a comment by James Moore of TSSWCB, not to an analysis. The article says "the state's own assessment judged."
- [^18] USGS Honey Creek: the "most of the savings in the first two years" point is the USGS report's summary of Dugas et al. 1998, not a USGS measurement. The article says "an earlier plot study summarized in the same report."
- [^19] Wilcox et al. 2014 is a public comment letter, not a peer-reviewed paper. It is used for the North Concho outcome and the small-spring point, both of which it attributes to published work (Wilcox et al. 2008, 2010; Huang et al. 2006; Owens et al. 2006). It is a credible, named, primary-author source, but the editor may prefer to flag it as such.
- [^20] Ansley et al. 2013: verified from the BioOne abstract only. The "most of the decline before 35%" detail from the snippet was not used; only the 30% threshold recommendation stated in the abstract.
- [^29] Lyons and Ginnett: the AgriLife page lists March 1998 and publication number RWFM-PU-037 (the newer numbering).
