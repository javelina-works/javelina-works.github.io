How it works

Three steps, and you approve the plan

How this page is lit

Fast enough for a phone

  • Every wall is lit on the graphics card by up to six lights at once: the lamp in the header, and the tube on the tin wall once it fills. Each wall’s bumps are drawn once; after that a single lighting pass runs, only while you scroll.
  • The top wall is the heavy, flaky rust; the tin wall is corrugated like the one in the bug photo; the bottom wall is the smoother raking light from the board.
  • Brass shows up in only two places: the clamps holding the tube and the plate under it. Each rivet has a glint per light, in that light’s color, sliding toward it. Card shadows are pre-blurred shapes that slide and fade, one per light.
  • The green pilot on the tube’s left clamp, and the status light at the foot of the page, are lights of a different color. The status light blinks slowly; its wall is redrawn only during the quarter-second fades, not between them. On rust green reads as a dim olive, because rust soaks up green; on the brass and where it overlaps the orange, it shifts toward yellow, the way real light adds.
  • The bands and the dark ground use light pools drawn once and slid into place. When the page is still, nothing runs. With reduced motion on, the lamp and tube are simply on.
  • By day (the switch at the bottom right) the sun lights every wall from the upper left, and all shadows fall down and to the right. The lamp only glows when you point at it, and its light barely shows against the sun, as it would outside.
