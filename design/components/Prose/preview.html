Texas Parks and Wildlife puts it plainly in its West Texas brush guidance: managers should avoid excessive removal of woody cover because “inadequate cover can be just as detrimental as too much brush.”1 If you want the broader case for brush work first, start with why brush control pays.

TPWD’s cover targets by species

TPWD’s Trans-Pecos guidelines give an optimum range of woody cover for long-term production and survival of each species:1

SpeciesOptimum woody cover
Pronghorn1–10%
Bobwhite quail5–25%
Scaled quail10–35%
Mule deer15–40%
Turkey40–60%
White-tailed deer50–75%

The intensity standard

The intensity standard reads:

Brush Management practices should annually affect a minimum of 10% of the total area designated in the plan, or a minimum of 100 acres annually, whichever is smaller.2

The math is simple:

  • 640-acre plan area. Ten percent is 64 acres. That is smaller than 100, so 64 acres a year meets the standard.
  • 1,000-acre plan area. Ten percent is 100 acres, the same as the floor.
