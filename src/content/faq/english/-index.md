    list:
      - active: true
        title: "What is the ballpark estimate?"
        content: |
          A free, roughly one-minute read of your ranch. Tell us where the fence line is — by address, by standing on it with your phone, or by drawing it — and we pull public satellite imagery and airborne lidar for that exact ground, measure the standing brush, and hand you a budget range for clearing it. It's a planning number, not a bid.
      - active: false
        title: "How accurate is it?"
        content: |
          Honest, but wide on purpose. The estimate comes from real measurements of your ground — laser terrain scans and infrared imagery — but from hundreds of feet up, not from a survey flight. We give you a range instead of a single number, and the width of that range reflects how good the public data is over your ranch. The firm quote comes later, from a scheduled survey flight that sees every individual plant.
      - active: false
        title: "What data do you use to read my ranch?"
        content: |
          Public, government-collected sources: USGS airborne lidar (laser terrain and vegetation-height scans), USDA aerial imagery including the near-infrared band that living plants light up in, and state parcel records to find your property lines. Exactly how we combine them into a brush count is our recipe — but every source is real data over your actual ground.
      - active: false
        title: "Is it really free? What's the catch?"
        content: |
          Free, no catch. You only hear from us if you leave your contact info asking for a firm quote. We built it because guessing at a clearing budget from the kitchen table was the hardest part of getting started.
      - active: false
        title: "What happens to my information?"
        content: |
          Your boundary and contact details are used to prepare your quote and nothing else. The analysis imagery we render for the map show is automatically deleted after about thirty days.
      - active: false
        title: "The map found the wrong property — now what?"
        content: |
          Property records in rural Texas can lag reality. You can tap neighboring parcels to add or remove them, size a simple circle around where you're standing, upload a boundary file if you have one, or just draw the fence line by hand.

  - label: "Clearing brush"
    list:
      - active: true
        title: "What kind of brush do you handle?"
        content: |
          Woody brush encroachment — juniper (cedar), mesquite, and the like. The plants that drink your water, crowd your grass, and spread a little further every year.
      - active: false
        title: "How is your approach different from dozers or chains?"
        content: |
          We map first and treat second. A survey flight photographs your ranch down to centimeters, our detection models find and count every individual plant, and treatment is targeted plant-by-plant from the air — precision work instead of scraping whole pastures, so your grass and soil stay where they are.
      - active: false
        title: "Why clear brush at all?"
        content: |
          Water, grass, and land value. Heavy brush intercepts rainfall before it reaches the water table, chokes out forage, and drags down carrying capacity. Clearing it is one of the highest-return improvements a working ranch can make.
      - active: false
        title: "How long does the work take?"
        content: |
          It scales with acreage and density — the ballpark estimate includes a rough "days on the ranch" figure for your ground, and the firm quote pins it down.

  - label: "From estimate to firm quote"

      - active: true
        title: "I like my ballpark number. What happens next?"

      - active: false
        title: "What is a survey flight?"
        content: |
          A drone survey of your ranch that captures imagery sharp enough to see individual plants — centimeter-level detail instead of the hundreds-of-feet-up public data the ballpark uses. From it we count and locate every stand of brush, which is what makes a firm, per-plant quote possible.
      - active: false
        title: "Where do you operate?"
        content: |
          We're a Texas outfit. The instant ballpark works for most ground in Texas; if you're near a border or out of state, reach out through the contact page and we'll tell you straight whether we can serve you.
      - active: false
        title: "How does the firm quote relate to the ballpark?"
        content: |
          Same shape, better data. The ballpark prices what public sensors can see; the survey flight replaces those readings with a plant-by-plant count of your actual brush. Most firm quotes land inside the ballpark range — that's the point of showing you a range in the first place.
